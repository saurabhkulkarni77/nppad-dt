THE PLANT: A NUCLEAR POWER PLANT YOU CAN WATCH

This page is a three dimensional model of a pressurized water reactor (PWR for short), the most common kind of nuclear power plant in the world. Drag the model to turn it around, and scroll or pinch to zoom in. The numbers on the model are real recorded measurements from accident runs in a computer simulator called PCTRAN, the same simulator that produced the study's data.

What to try: pick an accident run below and press PLAY. The clock runs, the measurements on the model change, and you can watch the accident unfold. The slider lets you jump to any moment in the run.

How to read the model: the reactor building (containment) is the clear shell on the left. Inside it sit the reactor vessel with its hot core, the pressurizer (which keeps the water pressure steady), the steam generator, and the pump that pushes hot water around the loop. Steam leaves the building, spins the turbine, and the generator turns that spin into electricity. The water is then cooled in the condenser and sent back. The vessel is cut open at the front so you can see the core; it glows brighter or dimmer with the recorded reactor power. Bright pulses move through the pipes to show the water and steam circulating; their speed follows the recorded flows, and the pulses themselves are not measurements. The model shows one measured value every 20 seconds of the run, always the recorded value itself, never a made up in between number. Two parts carry no number, the generator and the condenser, because the dataset records no measurement for them. Rather than invent numbers, they are labelled as having none.
